# Report page design

What the report page shows, in what order, and why. Researched 2026-11-11 from dashboard and cost-reporting guidance, the old `/spend` and `/today` skills, and the dataviz rules this repo's charts follow.

## What the research says

- **A number needs a comparison to mean anything.** A bare total doesn't say whether to act. Pair each headline number with a previous period, a pace, or a limit, so it reads as a verdict ([Tabular Editor on KPI cards](https://tabulareditor.com/blog/kpi-card-best-practices-dashboard-design), [Esri on effective dashboards](https://enterprise.arcgis.com/en/portal/10.6/use/author-effective-dashboards.htm)).
- **Few headline numbers, the most important biggest and first.** Eight to twelve cards compete for attention. Keep the headline row to four tiles or fewer, with one hero figure (Stephen Few, via the same sources).
- **Overview first, then zoom and filter, then details on demand.** Shneiderman's mantra: the page opens on the overview, the date range and grouping narrow it, and a click or hover gives the detail ([Shneiderman 1996](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~telea001/uploads/VACourse/Shneiderman96.pdf)). One filter row at the top scopes everything below it.
- **Cost reports answer four questions:** what was used, what it cost, why it changed, and what to do next. They also show how fresh the data is ([OneUptime on showback dashboards](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-07-21-cloud-showback-dashboard-metrics/markdown)).
- **Budget views project the current rate to the end of the period** and compare that with the limit. The projection is the number that changes behavior ([budget dashboard guide](https://www-infograph-ai.pages.dev/blog/budget-dashboard), [Datadog on cloud cost management](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/cloud-cost-management-finops)).
- **Stacked bars show the total and the leading part well, and middle parts poorly.** Cap the stack at the top series plus "other", and give exact comparisons to ranked bars or a table ([PolicyViz](https://policyviz.com/2025/06/24/can-we-improve-on-the-stacked-bar-chart/), [SAS on "Others"](https://blogs.sas.com/content/iml/2013/01/14/create-a-bar-chart-with-an-others-category.html)).
- **Tables carry the detail; bars inside rows show magnitude at a glance.** Right-aligned tabular figures and muted rules, with charts as summaries above the table, as Stripe's dashboard does ([Datawrapper on bars in tables](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/how-to-add-bar-charts-line-charts-to-tables), [Setproduct on data tables](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/data-table-ui-design)).
- **AI spend pages break cost down by model with the token mix beside it**, and separate rising volume from rising unit cost ([OpenRouter's activity page](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/activity-dashboard/), [Metabase's LLM spend overview](https://www.metabase.com/dashboards/llm-spend-overview)).

From the old skills, the ideas that held up are credits left and how long they last, credits per remaining weekday, and who did the work, meaning which subagent ran on which model.

## Layout

One page, read top to bottom, one account at a time.

When `gigawatts doctor` lists a problem, a box above the headline names what each one makes wrong and since when, with its cause, so a dip in a chart isn't taken at face value. The fix steps stay in `doctor`.

1. **Filter row.** One toolbar: an account picker (each tracked account with its spend today, plus All accounts, which the page opens on when more than one account is tracked), a range picker, any scope chips, a theme picker (Auto, Light, Dark, remembered in the browser), and Export. The range picker's button names the preset and its dates ("This month | Nov 1–11"), and its menu holds the presets with a custom from-and-to form below them. Account is context you switch rarely and range is a filter you change often, so both are menus behind one button each rather than tab bars, which keeps the row to one line on a phone. Everything below follows both. The view is kept in the address, so a reload or a bookmark returns to it.
2. **Headline.** Two rows, numbers and bars only, so it reads at a glance; the charts are further down.
   - *Money*, the first row: *Spend* for the range (the hero) and *Today*, side by side as two separate figures. In All accounts each has a bar split by account in each account's color, with every tracked account's dollars under it, $0 included, in the same order as the limits below. Spend's label names the range ("Spend, Nov 1–25"), and one quiet line under it gives the month-end pace on this month and the change against the previous period. This month compares with the same days of last month (Nov 1–11 with Oct 1–11), and last month with the whole month before, as monthly reporting does. Other ranges compare with the same number of days just before them. Every comparison names its dates. Shares, such as the cache-writes share in "What the money paid for", compare whenever the previous period has any data, since a missing day barely moves a share; dollar comparisons need the whole previous period in history. History counts as starting the day after the earliest record, because Claude Code's transcript cleanup usually cuts that first day part-way; a comparison that reaches further back says there is no comparison instead of showing a ratio. Today compares with the usual spend by this time of day, summed over the accounts in view, and warns at 1.5 times the usual.
   - *Limits*, the second row: one column per account in All accounts, ordered by spend, each listing every limit it has: weekly, any scoped weekly limit such as Fable, and the session limit when it is the closest or forecast to fill. The first limit in a column is large, the rest smaller. Each has its even-pace tick and, when it's forecast to fill, its 100% forecast; the first also shows its reset. A credit account's credits show what's used of the cap and what's left per weekday until the reset, or when they run out first. A single account spreads its limits across the row. The estimate of use elsewhere and where the reading came from sit in the limit's side panel; the session limit's estimate sits in each weekly limit's panel, beside its chart.
3. **What to change.** Unscoped page only. One card with three tabs, Suggestions, Anthropic's advice, and Changes, and a short subtitle beside the title that names the tab's time window, since none of them follows the range picker. Each row is a sentence with one number on the right; the other figures wait in the row's side panel. Each tab shows 3 rows, and "Show all N" lists the rest in place.
4. **Spend per day.** Stacked columns of the top 8 repos or models, plus "other", or of each account, the default in All accounts, with a hover readout of every series for that day. A legend is always shown, and the table below carries every value.
5. **Where it went** and **What the money paid for**, side by side, stacked on a narrow screen.
   - *Where it went*: repo, skill, subagent, or model, as ranked neutral bars with cost and share, repo and branch names in monospace. It shows the top 7 rows, and once 2 or more would be left over, they fold into one "Others (N)" row that carries their sum, so the rows still add up to the total. A fixed count keeps the card the same height from one range to the next. Its "Show" link lists them at the same level, with a "Show fewer" link under them. A click on any other row opens its side panel, which carries the row's sessions and what it paid for, and a branch is a breakdown inside its repo's scope.
   - *What the money paid for*: one 100% bar of input, output, cache writes, and cache reads at list price, then one row per token type with its dollars, its share, the previous period's share ("30% in October"), and one line on what drives it. Input is gray, since it is rarely more than a sliver. Cache writes is highlighted, since it is the part a habit changes most. Raw token counts are left out, because a cache-read token costs a twentieth of an input token and a hundredth of an output token, so counts mislead.
6. **Limits.** One row per limit (weekly, any scoped weekly limit such as Fable, and credits for a credit account), labeled by account in All accounts: this period's meter with its even-pace mark and reset, the highest reading in the last 4 weeks with its week (named by the day it starts, and marked "one reading" when only one reading backs it), and "Ran out", which says "Never" or how many periods reached 100% and the last date, beside a warning dot. Readings group into periods by their reset time rounded to the hour, and credits readings, which carry no reset time, by the account's `credit_reset` day, or by calendar month when none is set. Only readings from the last 4 weeks count. A click opens the limit's chart over the range in the side panel, with the session limit's chart under it; session limits appear only there. Limits belong to the account, so in a scope the card stays and says it covers the whole account.
7. **Who did the work.** Subagents (and your own conversation) by model, as a table of whole dollars with each row's total and share at the right. It shows at a glance when a subagent runs on the expensive model. On the page its rows fold the same way as Where it went, with the "Others (N)" row carrying only its total and share. The exported Markdown and PDF fold only rows under 1% of spend once there are more than 8, as the export's other tables do, and show that row without the link, and a total row.
8. **Biggest sessions.** The five most expensive sessions in the range, with "Show 5 more" in the card's header for the next five, and the columns Session, Where, Share, and Cost. The Where column drops out when every row shares the same repo and branch. A session row shows its title with its date, main skill, active time, and peak context, and opens a side panel with its split by skill, model, and subagent.

A past range (last month, or a custom range that ends before today) leads with that period instead of current state: its average active day, busiest day, and biggest driver.

## Details and scope

The page has two ways to look closer, for two different questions.

- **The side panel answers "what is this one thing?"** and leaves the page as it is, so you can open a row, close it, and open the next while comparing. A click on any row or on a day in the chart opens it. The panel shows the same parts for everything: the total against the previous period, a small trend over the range, what the cost paid for (input, output, cache writes, cache reads) with one sentence on what the largest part usually means, its split along the other groupings (a repo's skills and models, a skill's repos), and its top sessions. A split list shows up to 7 entries, largest first; with more, it shows 6 and the rest as one "Others (N)" line with their sum, or "Other" in a day's panel when the rest include the chart's own "Other". A day's panel compares it with the usual day. A session's panel stays as it is.
- **A scope answers "show me everything within this"** and redraws the whole page for it. The panel's *Focus on <name>* button applies it, and on a day it sets the range to that day. The scope shows as a chip in the filter row (`Repo: gigawatts ✕`). Scopes combine, such as a repo and a skill, and the address keeps them, so Back and bookmarks work.

The "Not in transcripts" row (spend Claude Code billed for requests it never wrote to its transcripts) opens a panel that explains it and shows its cost, its change against the period before, its days, and its split by model. It has no Focus button, since nothing can be filtered to it.

The "What to change" card sits under the headline, because what to act on matters more than the detail below it. Its three tabs come from two sources:

- *Suggestions* ("Last 8 weeks, all accounts"): one row per skill or subagent with a takeaway sized in dollars, the most at stake first. The title line is its biggest takeaway as a sentence that names it ("review launches 1.9 code-reviewers a run"). The line under it is what to check for a rise in cost per run or for first requests, and otherwise its runs and cost per run over the last 2 weeks. The right side is what it has at stake a month. A footer counts the rows that ran with nothing flagged. Suggestions is the default tab, because what to do next matters more than the history.
- *Anthropic's advice* ("This week, from /usage · shares overlap"): Anthropic's recommendations from Claude Code's `/usage`, which the engine saves hourly, in the order Claude Code gives them, with each one's share of the week's usage on the right. In All accounts one row carries every account's share, labeled by account. The tab shows only when the range includes today, rows open nothing, and it stays out of exports, because the advice names files and paths.
- *Changes* ("Last 8 weeks, all accounts"): did past changes make cost per run better or worse.

Suggestions and Changes come from `gigawatts insights`, so they cover the last 8 weeks across every account and ignore both the range and the account picker. The tab isn't called "Savings" because Changes leads with money already saved, and not "History" because it lists only changes of 30% or more, not every week. Skills and subagents share one Suggestions list, with "subagent" after a subagent's name, so the biggest saving leads whichever kind it is.

Changes is its own tab, not rows in Suggestions, because ranking by what's left to save sinks a change that already worked: the biggest saving in the history would sit low with nothing left to save. It opens on a totals strip, "Saved" and "Added" dollars a month, with saved in the status green. Below it is one row per change event, the largest first, with reversed ones last: the rows it moved and the cause as a sentence ("code-reviewer and security-reviewer moved to Opus 5.5, medium effort"), the week under it, and dollars a month saved or added on the right. An event that moved several rows, such as a skill and the subagent it launches, is one line, sized by the row it moved most, so nothing is counted twice. Rows join one event when they move the same way in the same week with the same model or effort change, or, with no such change, when one is a skill and the other a subagent it launches. A rise that a later change set back is folded into that later row ("undoing Oct 12: moved to Fable 5.1, high effort") instead of being listed on its own; with no such row it shows muted as "reversed <date>" in place of dollars. A rise counts as reversed when the same row, or any row it also moved, later switches the same model or effort back, or when a later drop on the same row brings cost per run back to where the rise started or below.

A click on a change opens its own panel: what it saves or costs, the next step ("Keep it", "setting it back would save about $X a month", or, with no model or effort cause, which skill's edits to check around that date), then a weekly cost-per-run chart for each row it moved, with the weeks before the change faded, a dot on the change week and on the week of any rise it undid, and the two-week averages on each side beside the row's name, and a link to each row's panel. The charts are the point of the panel: a change is something that happened over time, and two averages alone can't show whether it held.

Rows in Suggestions are ranked by what they have at stake, the way cost tools rank recommendations by dollar impact (Datadog's recommendations, Google's Active Assist, AWS anomaly root causes). A row has no trend chart: at row height a trend has no scale and low-run weeks make it look busy, so the 8-week detail lives in the panel. A finding about a subagent a skill launches ("1.8 code-reviewer launches a run") stays on the skill, which decides how many to launch. A skill's cost already includes the subagents it launched, so a skill and one of its subagents can count the same dollars.

A click opens the row's side panel: its numbers; its cost per run as one bar a week for 8 weeks, with the top of the scale in dollars, each week's run count under its bar, a dot on each week a change started, and a hover readout of the week's cost per run, runs, and total; its takeaways, each a title, its dollars a month, and what to check; its changes, the only place a row's own changes are listed, each the date and cause, what it saves or costs a month or "reversed <date>", the two-week averages on each side, and the other rows the same change moved; the subagents it launches; and a prompt to copy into Claude for the edits. Takeaways and changes come from fixed rules over the insights numbers, computed by `insights::takeaways` and served with the insights numbers by the private `/api/insights` route. The takeaways are: repeat runs at 25% or more of the cost, 1.5 or more launches of one subagent a run, first requests at 25% or more of a subagent's cost, a costliest tenth of sessions at half the cost or more over at least 10 sessions, and a rise in cost per run that no later change reversed. Anything under $20 a month is left out, and a row with fewer than 10 runs says it's too thin to judge. A Claude Code version change is never named as a cause, since Claude Code updates every few days. The page says where to look; the edit itself needs your skill files, which only Claude reads.

Suggestions and Changes always cover the default 8 weeks, whatever the range picker says, because a shift needs four weeks to compare. The card is left out of a scoped page, since its runs can't be split by repo, and out of exports, because it always covers the default 8 weeks across every account and would disagree with the range and scope the export states.

A click previews and the button commits, because redrawing the page on every click gets in the way of scanning. A session is never a scope: it is the last level, and its panel already shows everything about it.

Within a scope the page changes to fit it:

| Scope | Headline tiles | Chart stacks by | Breakdown tabs (first is default) |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | As in Layout | Account (All accounts only), repo, or model | Repo, skill, subagent, model |
| Repo | Spend against the previous period, share of the account's spend, sessions, cost per session | Branch | Branch, skill, subagent, model |
| Skill | Same | Repo | Repo, subagent, model |
| Model | Same | Repo | Repo, skill, subagent; "Who did the work" leads, since it shows who chose the model |
| Subagent | Same | Model | Model, repo, skill |

Credits, limits, and month pace belong to the account, not to a scope, so in a scope they leave the headline and become one line under the filter row ("Account: $412 this month, 62% of credits"). The Limits card stays and says it covers the whole account. A scoped "Biggest sessions" lists that scope's sessions.

Scope needs the API to filter by any grouping, not only down a grouping's own levels: `repo`, `branch`, `skill`, `model`, and `agent` parameters on `/api/data`, `/api/series`, `/api/mix`, `/api/sessions`, and `/api/agents`.

## Export

Export shares a summary and leaves out repo, branch, and session names; skill, subagent, and model names stay, since you name those. A scoped view exports with its scope named in the title, so a repo's name appears only when you scoped to it. Two outputs:

- *Markdown*: the summary as text and tables. It pastes cleanly into Slack, GitHub, and Linear, and it is the best input for an agent, since models read exact values from a table more reliably than from a chart image ([Format Matters, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10075v1)).
- *PDF* through the browser's print dialog and a print stylesheet: the shareable picture, charts included, since they are SVG.

Both carry the same summary, written for someone judging the period rather than watching today: spend against the period before, active days with the average active day and the busiest one, sessions with cost per session, and credits used on a credit plan or otherwise how much input came from the prompt cache. Then spend per day by model, tables by model and by skill with each row's change against the period before, and who did the work. Today, limit forecasts, and the busiest session are left out, because they describe this moment rather than the period. A footnote explains spend that is not in transcripts.

A PNG is left out. Drawing the page to an image takes either an SVG `foreignObject`, which Safari renders unreliably, or a library, which the page otherwise avoids. Claude Enterprise admins already get per-user, per-model spend from Anthropic's analytics and spend report; this adds why the money went where it did.

## Rules

- Colors follow the dataviz reference palette, validated in light and dark (`validate_palette.js`: all checks pass, with three light colors under 3:1 relieved by the table and legend). Status colors (good, warning, critical) appear on meters, always beside a number. Text in the good color, such as saved dollars, uses its own `--good` token per theme, because the chart green is about 2.6:1 on white.
- A series keeps its color everywhere on the page: the chart and its legend. An account keeps its avatar's color in the chart and the spend split. The "Where it went" bars are neutral, since their length and figures carry the comparison.
- One axis per chart. Gridlines are solid hairlines. Bars are at most 24 px wide, with a 2 px gap between stacked parts.
- Estimates say "about" ("On pace for about $841 this month", "Runs out about Dec 3"). The headline leaves out what they're based on to stay numbers-only; a limit's side panel says which pace its forecast uses.
- The page reads only the engine's own API on 127.0.0.1, and inserts all data as text.
